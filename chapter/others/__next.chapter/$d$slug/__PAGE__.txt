5:T5a5,[DIAGNOSIS]
- Pallor, fatigue, breathlessness, dizziness
- Lab: Low haemoglobin, low ferritin, low MCV (microcytic)
- Rule out occult blood loss before starting iron

[INVESTIGATIONS]
- Lab: FBC (haemoglobin, MCV low), ferritin (low in iron deficiency), iron studies, B12/folate
- Special test: FOBT (faecal occult blood) or colonoscopy if occult GI bleeding suspected (men/postmenopausal)
- Monitor: Hb response (rise ~1g/dL in 2-4 weeks), ferritin repletion, GI side effects of oral iron
- Screen: Underlying cause — GI source, menstrual loss, malabsorption (coeliac)

[TREATMENT]
- Oral iron is first line for most cases
- Parenteral iron (IV) only if oral fails or not tolerated
- Combine with vitamin C for better absorption
- Avoid concurrent intake with PPIs, tea, milk, or calcium

[WARNING]
Iron overdose is dangerous — keep away from children. Do not exceed recommended dose.
Iron stains teeth — use a straw for liquid forms.
