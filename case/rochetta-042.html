القلب والأوعية الدموية

Heart Failure

Dyspnea, edema, fatigue — reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF)

تحذير
Beta-blockers in HF: Start at lowest dose, titrate every 2 weeks. Do NOT stop abruptly. Use only Carvedilol, Bisoprolol, or Metoprolol succinate.
  • - SOB on exertion, orthopnea, PND
  • - Bilateral pitting pedal edema
  • - BNP/NT-proBNP elevated — confirms diagnosis
  • - Lab: BNP/NT-proBNP (elevated), renal function, electrolytes, LFTs, full blood count
  • - Imaging: Echocardiogram — ejection fraction, chamber size, valvular function
  • - ECG: Baseline 12-lead — LVH, ischaemia, arrhythmia, QRS duration
  • - Monitor: Daily weight, electrolytes/K+ (RAAS blockade), renal function, potassium
  • - ACEi/ARB/ARNI + Beta-blocker + MRA = Foundation
  • - Add SGLT2i (Empagliflozin / Dapagliflozin) — mortality benefit
  • - Diuretic for congestion (Furosemide PRN)
  • - Start low, titrate to target doses — 4 pillars save lives

️ Weigh yourself DAILY — report gain >2kg in a week
️ Ramipril: Start low, titrate slowly
️ Carvedilol: Take WITH food
️ Furosemide: Take in the MORNING — avoid nighttime urination
️ Limit salt to <6g/day
️ Limit fluid intake to 1.5-2L/day if instructed
Do NOT stop Beta-blocker suddenly
Do NOT take NSAIDs (Ibuprofen) — worsens HF
️ Report worsening breathlessness, increased swelling, or weight gain immediately
↑ Hyperkalemia risk. Monitor K+ regularly.
Hypokalemia from Furosemide increases Digoxin toxicity. Monitor K+.
Severe hyperkalemia. Avoid K+ supplements.
Reduced efficacy + worsened renal function. Avoid.
