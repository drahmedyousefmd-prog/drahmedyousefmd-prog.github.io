المسالك البولية

Lower UTI / Cystitis

Dysuria, frequency, urgency — most common in women

تحذير
Pyelonephritis in pregnancy → URGENT hospital admission + IV antibiotics. No clinical improvement in 48–72h → re-culture, re-examine, re-image — do not simply switch antibiotics. Nitrofurantoin and Fosfomycin have poor renal-tissue penetration — not for pyelonephritis.
  • - Dysuria, urinary frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain
  • - Cloudy / malodorous urine
  • - Urine dipstick: positive for leukocytes + nitrites
  • - Uncomplicated — no fever, no flank pain
  • - Urine analysis (routine urine): WBCs (pus cells), RBCs, bacteria — pyuria supports UTI
  • - Urine dipstick: leukocyte esterase and nitrite — quick bedside screen; negative nitrite does NOT exclude UTI
  • - Urine culture + sensitivity (MSU): for recurrent, complicated, pregnancy, pyelonephritis, treatment failure
  • - Renal function (Urea, Creatinine): guides antibiotic choice and dose adjustment
  • - CBC / CRP: if systemic infection or fever suspected
  • - Blood cultures: if high fever, rigors, or sepsis suspected
  • - Imaging (US KUB / CT): only if suspected obstruction, stone, abscess, recurrent complicated UTI, or no fever response at 48–72h
  • - 1st line: Nitrofurantoin 100mg twice daily × 5 days
  • - OR Fosfomycin 3g single dose — convenient option
  • - Alternative (culture-guided): TMP-SMX if local resistance < 20%, or oral beta-lactam
  • - Fluoroquinolones are NOT first-line for uncomplicated UTI — reserve for complicated cases after culture
  • - Phenazopyridine: symptom relief only, ≤ 2–3 days
  • - Complicated / systemic: Ciprofloxacin or Ceftriaxone — usually 10–14 days
  • - Pregnancy: Nitrofurantoin (avoid near term ≥ 37 weeks and G6PD deficiency), Fosfomycin, or beta-lactam per culture
