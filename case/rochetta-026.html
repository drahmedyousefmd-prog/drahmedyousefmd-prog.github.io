Atopic Dermatitis

Pruritic, erythematous, scaly plaques — relapsing course

  • - Intense pruritus — worsening at night
  • - Flexural involvement (antecubital, popliteal)
  • - Asthma / allergic rhinitis association (atopic triad)
  • - Lab: CBC (eosinophilia), IgE level, FBS — screen for atopy
  • - Special test: Skin prick testing or specific IgE for allergen identification; TARC level
  • - Monitor: EASI/SCORAD score, SCORING quality of life, infection markers (secondary infection)
  • - Culture: Skin swab if secondary bacterial infection suspected (weeping, crusting)
  • - Emollients — foundation (daily)
  • - Mild TCS: Hydrocortisone 1% (acute flares)
  • - Moderate TCS: Betamethasone (severe)
  • - Calcineurin inhibitors: Tacrolimus (face)

49 ج.م
️ Emollients: Apply liberally and frequently — foundation of treatment
️ Hydrocortisone 1%: Apply thin layer, affected areas only, 7-14 days max
️ Betamethasone: Potent — avoid face, groin, axillae
️ Tacrolimus: Safe for face and flexures — apply thin layer twice daily
️ Avoid long-term continuous steroid use on same area (skin thinning)
Apply emollient first, wait 10 min, then steroid
️ Wet wrap therapy can enhance absorption in severe flares
Avoid known irritants: soap, fragrances, wool
↑ GI ulcer risk if on systemic steroids. Rare with topical alone.
Avoid live vaccines during treatment.
May increase systemic steroid effects if applied widely.
