الجهاز التنفسي

Bronchial Asthma

Reversible airway obstruction — wheeze, dyspnea, chest tightness

تحذير
Never stop ICS abruptly — taper under supervision. Uncontrolled asthma is the most common cause of preventable respiratory death.
  • - Episodic wheeze, dyspnea, chest tightness — worse at night
  • - Reversible obstruction on spirometry (≥12% improvement post-BD)
  • - Response to bronchodilator confirms diagnosis
  • - Spirometry: Reversible obstruction — ≥ 12% and ≥ 200mL FEV1 improvement post-bronchodilator
  • - Special test: Peak expiratory flow (PEF) variability; FeNO if available (eosinophilic)
  • - Imaging: Chest X-ray — exclude other causes of wheeze (first diagnosis)
  • - Monitor: Asthma control test (ACT), PEF diary, symptoms, rescue inhaler use frequency
  • - Step 1: SABA as needed (Salbutamol PRN)
  • - Step 2: Low-dose ICS (Budesonide / Fluticasone)
  • - Step 3: ICS + LABA (Formoterol / Salmeterol)
  • - Step 4: High-dose ICS + LABA + LTRA

️ ICS (Budesonide/Fluticasone): Daily PREVENTIVE use — NOT rescue
️ RINSE mouth after EVERY steroid inhaler — prevents oral thrush
️ Salbutamol (Ventolin): RESCUE only — if >2 times/week, see doctor
️ Never stop ICS abruptly — risk of severe attack
️ Carry rescue inhaler at ALL times
️ Check inhaler technique regularly
Budesonide: 1-2 puffs morning and evening
Salbutamol: 1-2 puffs when needed (max 8/day)
Avoid known triggers: dust, smoke, cold air
️ Written Asthma Action Plan recommended
May worsen bronchospasm. Use cardioselective beta-blocker only if needed.
