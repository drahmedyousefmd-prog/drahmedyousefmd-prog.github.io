Herpes Zoster

Unilateral dermatomal vesicular rash — VZV reactivation

تحذير
Immunocompromised or ophthalmic zoster → urgent referral. Avoid topical steroids on active lesions. PHN risk rises with age — treat pain early and aggressively.
  • - Unilateral dermatomal vesicular rash on erythematous base
  • - Burning/neuralgic pain precedes rash by 2-3 days
  • - Does not cross midline — follows single dermatome
  • - Ophthalmic zoster (V1): urgent referral if Hutchinson sign
  • - Culture: PCR from vesicle fluid or DFA to confirm VZV if atypical
  • - Lab: CBC, FBS, renal function — especially in immunocompromised (renal dosing of acyclovir)
  • - Special test: Slit-lamp examination if V1 (ophthalmic) involvement; HIV screen if severe/recurrent
  • - Monitor: Pain score (PHN risk), lesion healing, signs of secondary bacterial infection
  • - Start antivirals within 72h of rash onset — best outcome
  • - Analgesia essential — lowers post-herpetic neuralgia risk
  • - Topical care of vesicles — prevent secondary bacterial infection
