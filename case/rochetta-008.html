Epilepsy

Recurrent unprovoked seizures — AED selection by seizure type

تحذير
Key counseling points: Do NOT stop AEDs suddenly — risk of rebound seizures. Take at the same time daily. Avoid driving until seizure-free ≥ 1 year. Folic acid for women of childbearing age.
  • - Focal (partial): onset in one hemisphere — automatisms
  • - Generalized tonic-clonic: bilateral convulsions
  • - Absence: brief staring spells — children
  • - Status epilepticus: prolonged seizure > 5 min → emergency
  • - Lab: FBS, electrolytes (Na+, Ca2+, Mg2+), CBC, renal function, AED drug levels
  • - Imaging: MRI brain — identify structural lesions, tumour, vascular malformation
  • - ECG: Baseline — exclude arrhythmia as seizure mimic (QT prolongation)
  • - Monitor: AED levels (carbamazepine, valproic acid), seizure frequency diary
  • - Focal seizures: Carbamazepine / Levetiracetam / Lamotrigine
  • - Generalized tonic-clonic: Valproic acid / Lamotrigine / Levetiracetam
  • - Absence: Ethosuximide 1st line / Valproic acid
  • - Start monotherapy — lowest effective dose

️ NEVER stop AEDs suddenly — risk of seizures and status epilepticus
️ Take at the same time every day — consistent blood levels crucial
️ Avoid driving until seizure-free for at least 1 year
️ Report any rash immediately — especially with Lamotrigine
️ Women of childbearing age: take Folic Acid 5mg daily
Take with food to reduce GI upset
Avoid alcohol — lowers seizure threshold
️ Wear medical alert identification
Strong CYP450 inducer — dramatically reduces effectiveness of many drugs including OCPs.
Risk of severe skin rash (SJS). Titrate Lamotrigine very slowly.
Reduces effectiveness. Alternative contraception needed.
